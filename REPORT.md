# Report

The study report in the portfolio standard's sense (this file was
AUDIT.md, the author's audit; the locked PREREGISTRATION.md does not
name it, so it takes the standard's name). State, 2026-11-08: both
arms ran on all 50 FUNSD test documents under the frozen protocol;
RESULTS.md and results/metrics.json are generated from the raw
records; CI regenerates them and fails on any difference.

## Background

### Problem

Every document-processing team faces the same fork: build on
classical OCR plus rules you control, or send pages to a vision
model and take the extraction it gives you. The claims made for
both routes are usually benchmark-free. This study measures the
fork on real forms, with both arms scored against the same
human-annotated ground truth, under rules frozen before any run.

### Purpose

Form and document extraction is the core workload of
document-processing roles (the Scotiabank Global AI posting that
motivated this lab names it directly). This repo is the measured
evidence for that workload: what each route gets right, what it
gets wrong, and what it costs in time and reliability.

### Why this matters beyond the lab

Forms are how banks, insurers, and governments ingest the paper
world: account openings, claims, permits, benefits. What gets
automated safely is decided by measured error rates, not by
demos. A pipeline that misreads one token in fifteen can still
be the right choice where a human checks every field, and a
model that reads beautifully but is refused by its own access
route three times in five can stall a digitization programme
that never priced that in. Knowing both numbers, in the open,
is what lets a small team choose the boring, reliable option
where boring and reliable is what the work needs.

## Method

The protocol is PREREGISTRATION.md, locked in commit 0774bfd before
any code or run (tag proposal-v1). In short:

- Corpus: FUNSD, the 50-document published test split, dataset.zip
  verified by SHA-256 at download; never committed.
- Arm A: Tesseract 5.3.4 (user-space install, see DECISIONS.md
  D1) produces one TSV pass per document; a deterministic rule
  stage (colon split, next-line value, wide-gap segment; rules
  R1 to R3 in PREREGISTRATION.md, with amendment A1) emits
  key-value pairs. No learned model.
- Arm B: Gemini 3 Flash Preview, reached only through the
  existing connected Gemini connector (custom.legacy-gemini),
  one vision call per document with a frozen prompt, temperature
  0, one retry on error, failures counted.
- Scoring is shared code (src/common.py): ground-truth pairs
  from FUNSD linking annotations, frozen normalization,
  greedy multiset matching, micro-averaged precision, recall,
  F1; WER by token Levenshtein against annotation word order.
- Machine of record: the Muse VM (htch-runtime), named in the
  protocol. No number here comes from any other machine.

## Results

Generated by scripts/make_results.py into RESULTS.md and
results/metrics.json; the per-document table is in RESULTS.md.

| Measure | Arm A: Tesseract + rules | Arm B: Gemini vision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field precision (micro) | 0.0941 | 0.7865 |
| Field recall (micro) | 0.0490 | 0.2708 |
| Field F1 (micro) | 0.0645 | 0.4029 |
| True positives / predicted / ground truth | 40 / 425 / 816 | 221 / 281 / 816 |
| Median seconds per document | 3.583 | 7.462 |

Every hypothesis, scored against its frozen pass line:

- H1 CONFIRMED: the vision-LLM arm beats the classical arm on
  field F1 by 0.3385 (0.33845 unrounded), against a pre-registered
  line of 0.15. (The old README printed 0.3384, the difference of
  the two rounded F1 values; RESULTS.md's 0.3385 is the generated
  figure.)
- H2 CONFIRMED: the classical arm's OCR word error rate is
  0.6942 (6060 edits over 8729 reference tokens), against a
  pre-registered line of 0.10.
- H3 INCONCLUSIVE: the classical arm is faster per document
  (median 3.583 s vs 7.462 s, a ratio of 2.08), but not by the
  pre-registered factor of 5, and not by zero either. This is the
  miss: the registered claim did not hold. See the latency note
  under "Interpretation notes" before quoting it either way.

The number that conditions everything else: the vision route
answered only 21 of the 50 documents. The other 29 failed under
the frozen failure rule and scored zero predicted pairs: 27 were
HTTP 429 rate-limit rejections from the access route, 2 were
call timeouts (89 calls in all). On the 21 documents the route did
answer (294 ground-truth pairs), its descriptive F1 is 0.7687 and
recall 0.7517, against the classical arm's F1 0.0628 on the same
documents. These subset figures are descriptive, not registered
outcomes; make_results.py does not write them, and the one-liner in
"How to re-derive the descriptive figures" below recomputes them
from results/arm_b.jsonl and results/metrics.json.

### Verdict in words

On accuracy the vision route wins by a wide margin wherever it
answers at all; the classical pipeline as specified here is not
close (F1 0.0645). On reliability the classical pipeline is the
predictable one: it processed all 50 documents on local hardware,
while the vision route, submitted in a burst through the existing
access route, was rate-limited into failing 29 of 50. On latency
neither registered outcome holds: the classical arm is about twice
as fast at the median, not five times, and the vision arm's median
is itself mostly a measurement of fast rate-limit rejections, so
H3 is honestly inconclusive.

## Error character

Arm A fails in three visible ways. Its OCR text on these
degraded fax-era scans is noisy at the character level
("614-~466-S087" for a fax number). Its rule stage then accepts
pairs whose values are whole line remainders, while the ground
truth's values are annotation blocks that often span several
lines, so most of its 425 predictions cannot match exactly under
the frozen normalization. And its word error rate of 0.6942
counts more than misread characters: the reference token order
is annotation order, not reading order, and the two tokenize
numbers and punctuation differently. A diagnostic on document
82092117 (token multiset overlap 133 of 223 against the
reference) suggests ordering and tokenization divergence is a
large share of the distance, alongside genuine character noise.
The 0.6942 stands as the frozen metric defines it; the
diagnostic is context, not a correction. (The diagnostic's
script is not in the repo; only its result is recorded here.)

Arm B fails differently: cleanly, at the service boundary. Its
extraction quality on answered documents is high (precision
0.7865 overall, recall 0.7517 on answered documents) and its
errors are transcription choices, not noise. Its dominant
failure mode in this study is not extraction at all but access:
submitted back to back, the route returned HTTP 429 for most
documents after the first dozen. A production version of this
arm is a pacing and quota problem before it is a modelling
problem. That is a finding about the route as accessed, stated
as such; this study did not re-run under friendlier pacing,
because the pacing was part of the frozen protocol.

## How the evidence is protected

1. Pre-registration before results. PREREGISTRATION.md is the
   first content commit (0774bfd), written before any code or
   run existed. Git history shows the order; the tag proposal-v1
   marks it, and tests/test_layout.py pins its SHA-256.
2. Numbers come from scripts. Every registered number in README.md
   and RESULTS.md is computed by scripts/make_results.py from
   results/arm_a.jsonl and results/arm_b.jsonl via the shared
   scorer in src/common.py. Verdicts are computed from the
   frozen pass lines by that script, never typed by hand. CI
   regenerates both outputs and fails on any difference.
3. Failures are data, not inconveniences. Arm B failed 29 of 50
   documents (27 HTTP 429 rate-limit rejections, 2 timeouts).
   Every failure is a raw record with its error string, scored
   as zero predicted pairs, and kept in every denominator,
   exactly as the pre-registration froze it. No document was
   excluded for being hard, and Arm B was not re-run under
   gentler pacing to improve its numbers: the burst submission
   and the one-retry rule were the protocol.
4. Corpus integrity. FUNSD dataset.zip, SHA-256
   c31735649e4f441bcbb4fd0f379574f7520b42286e80b01d80b445649d54761f,
   verified at download by scripts/fetch_funsd.sh; the dataset
   is never committed (data/ is gitignored). Scoring covers the
   published 50-document test split only.
5. Arms as registered. Arm A is the real Tesseract 5.3.4 (see
   DECISIONS.md D1 for the user-space install) plus the frozen
   rules; Arm B used only the existing connected Gemini route
   (custom.legacy-gemini), the model it serves
   (gemini-3-flash-preview, confirmed in response metadata and
   results/run_manifest.json), the frozen prompt, temperature 0.
   No new key, account, or provider was created.
6. Spend. Zero. Tesseract is open source, the corpus is public,
   and the Gemini calls went through the user's existing
   connected route.
7. Jev claim_check. Model jev-1.13.0, question "supported",
   noul 0.47. Under the claim_check bands (clear at 0.85 or
   above, block at 0.10 or below, review between), the route is
   REVIEW. The claim was therefore hand-verified figure by
   figure against results/metrics.json and the raw JSONL: F1
   values 0.4029 and 0.0645 and their difference; WER
   0.6942 as 6060 edits over 8729 reference tokens; medians
   3.58 s and 7.46 s and the H3 arithmetic (5 x 3.58 = 17.9
   exceeds 7.46; 3.58 is below 7.46); the 29 failures split as
   27 HTTP 429 and 2 timeouts across 89 total calls; the
   answered-subset descriptors (21 documents, 294 ground-truth
   pairs, Arm B F1 0.7687, Arm A F1 0.0628). Every figure in the
   claim agrees with the records. Spec and response are in
   results/jev-claim-spec.json and results/jev-claim-check.json
   (1102 input tokens, 20 output tokens).

## Deviations and incidents on record

- DECISIONS.md D1: Tesseract is a user-space install (Ubuntu
  packages unpacked under .tools/), because this VM ships no
  system Tesseract and no sudo. The arm is still the named
  engine, version recorded in the run manifest.
- Amendment A1 (before any measured run): R2's value search
  stopped at the first intervening key line, after a unit test
  showed the original text could steal a later key's value.
  Recorded in DECISIONS.md; no run precedes it.
- DECISIONS.md D2: during the Arm B run, one call sat in flight
  far past the intended timeout (a urllib socket timeout does
  not bound a slow-drip response through the proxy). The client
  gained a hard 180-second per-attempt deadline enforced on a
  daemon thread; exceeding it is the timeout the frozen failure
  rule already describes. The stuck attempt produced no record;
  the affected document's recorded outcome (two deadline
  expiries, counted failed) came from the corrected client.
- DECISIONS.md D3: after the results commit, the credential-helper
  import was guarded so the tests import on a clean CI runner. No
  run record changed.
- The HTTP 429 pattern: successes and failures interleave early
  (first failure at document position 2, a timeout), and
  rejections dominate from about the fourteenth document on
  (positions 14 to 16 are the first run of 429s in
  results/arm_b.jsonl). This is reported as the route's behavior
  under burst submission, not smoothed into a smaller failure
  count.

## Interpretation notes (not protocol outputs)

- The WER reference order is FUNSD annotation order, which is
  not reading order, and the annotation tokenization splits
  numbers and punctuation that Tesseract emits as single tokens.
  A one-document diagnostic (82092117: token multiset overlap
  133 of 223) indicates ordering and tokenization divergence is
  a large share of the 0.6942, alongside genuine character
  noise on degraded scans. H2's verdict is unaffected (the line
  was 0.10), but readers should not quote 0.6942 as a pure
  character-error rate.
- Arm B's all-document median time (7.46 s) is dominated by
  fast rejections (median time of the 27 HTTP 429 failures
  7.05 s, two quick 429 responses plus the frozen 5-second pause;
  over all 29 failed documents, including the two timeouts, the
  median is 7.08 s). The answered documents' median call time is
  23.32 s. H3's INCONCLUSIVE verdict is computed exactly as
  frozen, over all 50 records per arm; as a pure speed comparison
  it is weak evidence.

## Threats to validity

- Arm B coverage. The route answered 21 of 50 documents; the
  study's central comparison is therefore rate-limit-bound. The
  failures are honestly counted and are themselves a finding,
  but nearly three fifths of the vision arm's cells are access
  rejections, not extractions, and no verdict here should be
  read as a clean evaluation of the model's ceiling.
- The H3 latency measurement is contaminated by those
  rejections (fast failures pull Arm B's median down), so the
  latency verdict carries weak information despite being
  computed exactly as registered.
- Arm A's extractor is one minimal deterministic ruleset. Its
  results bound that ruleset, not classical OCR pipelines in
  general; the bound narrows what the study proves.
- The frozen WER counts reading-order and tokenization
  divergence as error, so the headline 0.6942 overstates pure
  character error by an amount this study diagnoses on one
  document but does not quantify across the set.
- One corpus, one vision model, one pass per document. Arm B has
  no repeat runs, so its run-to-run variance is unmeasured, and
  no headline number carries an interval.
- The Arm B client was corrected mid-run (D2). Failure semantics
  were unchanged and the incident is documented, but a stricter
  protocol would have restarted the arm from zero under the
  corrected client.
- No ablation and no stronger classical or layout-aware baseline
  (for example a learned layout model) was run. The ML field
  profile asks for both, which is why the verdict is Functional,
  not Evaluated.

## What the study does show

A genuinely locked pre-registration with decidable pass lines;
both arms executed in full on the published test split; an
inconvenient failure mode (the sponsor route's own rate limit)
measured, counted, and published rather than routed around;
verdicts and every registered headline number generated by script
from raw records; and a claim that survived a Jev review route by
hand-verification against those records.

## Conclusion

Where the vision route answers, it extracts form fields far more
accurately than the registered classical pipeline (H1 confirmed);
the classical OCR output on these scans is noisy by the frozen
measure (H2 confirmed); and the latency claim did not hold as
registered (H3 inconclusive). The practical finding is
reliability: under burst submission the vision route failed 29 of
50 documents at the access layer. Lifting the verdict to Evaluated
needs a newly locked confirmatory cycle with intervals (repeat
runs or a document-level bootstrap), ablations, a paced Arm B, and
a stronger classical baseline; that cycle calls the Gemini route
and waits for the Owner.

## How to re-derive the descriptive figures

From the committed records alone (no corpus needed), the
answered-subset and failure figures above come from:

```text
python3 -c "import json,statistics as s;m=json.load(open('results/metrics.json'));b=[json.loads(l) for l in open('results/arm_b.jsonl')];f=[r for r in b if r['failed']];ok={r['doc'] for r in b if not r['failed']};R=[r for r in m['rows'] if r['doc'] in ok];n=sum(r['truth'] for r in R);F=lambda t,p:2*t/(p+n);print(len(f),sum('429' in r['error'] for r in f),sum(r['calls'] for r in b),len(R),n,round(F(sum(r['b_tp'] for r in R),sum(r['b_pred'] for r in R)),4),round(F(sum(r['a_tp'] for r in R),sum(r['a_pred'] for r in R)),4),round(s.median(r['seconds'] for r in f if '429' in r['error']),2),round(s.median(r['seconds'] for r in b if not r['failed']),2))"
```

Expected output: `29 27 89 21 294 0.7687 0.0628 7.05 23.32`.
